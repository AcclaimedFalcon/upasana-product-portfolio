Eldercare — make changes from baseline visible earlier.

An early-stage AgeTech concept exploring pendant-based movement monitoring to help families and clinicians notice sustained changes in an older adult’s daily routine — without turning monitoring into diagnosis or surveillance.

EARLY-STAGE PRODUCT CONCEPT

04 / ELDERCARE

Earlier clarity, without burdening the people involved.

The concept starts with a human and systems question: how might we make early changes in an older adult's daily routine more visible, without burdening the person wearing the device or overwhelming caregivers and clinicians?

The gap

Care can be episodic — visits, vitals and calls — while monitoring is often reactive to falls, SOS events or hospitalisation. Information is fragmented across family observations, clinical notes and medication lists. A recurring question is: “What changed, and when?”

Primary hypothesis: If sustained deviations from an individual's personal baseline can be detected and presented clearly, caregivers and clinicians may gain earlier context and make more informed decisions. This is about visibility, not diagnosis; patterns, not predictions; supporting people, not replacing them.

Proposed experience

A lightweight, waterproof pendant passively captures movement data, learns an individual's usual patterns and surfaces meaningful deviations through a platform for caregivers and clinicians. The concept deliberately avoids automatic emergency dispatch, medical claims and autonomous clinical decisions.

V1: start with simple signals

  • Accelerometer and gyroscope in a comfortable, waterproof pendant.
  • Movement events, inactivity periods and posture/orientation changes.
  • Daily movement, sit-to-stand transitions and night-time movement frequency.
  • Potential fall-like impact followed by no recovery, and missed routine movement windows.
  • Build a personal baseline over an initial 7–10-day period, then flag sustained deviations.

These are functional movement signals, not medical vitals. No heavy machine learning is required for the first version. Medication or clinical-record data is context for clinician interpretation, not an independent trigger or instruction.

Illustrative scenario

After a baseline period, the system notices a sustained drop in movement and increased night-time movement compared with the individual's usual pattern. A caregiver sees a plain-language change summary; a clinician can review a trend against baseline and relevant context, such as a recent medication timing change. A person then decides whether a check-in or further review is appropriate.

AI boundaries

  • No disease prediction or autonomous decisions in V1.
  • No emergency automation.
  • Future ML could improve baseline learning, reduce false positives and identify multi-signal drift, but remains assistive.
  • A future workflow assistant could prepare summaries and suggest questions for review, not make clinical decisions.

Risks and proposed pilot

Wear adherence, false alerts, clinician data overload and hardware/operational complexity are central risks. Proposed mitigations include comfort-first design, conservative deviation logic, interpreted insights rather than raw-data overload and pilot-led iteration.

The concept note proposes a 30–50-person pilot over 2–3 months with home use and clinical oversight. Measures include wear adherence, false-alert rate, clinician usefulness feedback, interpretation time and caregiver confidence. These are proposed study parameters, not a completed pilot.

What this work demonstrates

Hypothesis-led concept development, baseline-relative product logic, human-centred monitoring, responsible AI boundaries, risk identification and a measurable feasibility plan.

Status: Concept stage only. Hardware, software, feasibility and clinical validation are not yet established. The idea is about supporting independence and dignity, not surveillance.
