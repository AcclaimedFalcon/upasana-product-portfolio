Arog — simplifying the clinical journey.

A healthcare product strategy and roadmap case study focused on reducing clinician cognitive load across the outpatient encounter — from the daily queue to patient context, consultation and sign-off.

PRODUCT STRATEGY CASE STUDY

02 / AROG

Make the encounter feel connected, not fragmented.

Arog's opportunity is not simply to add more functionality. It is to connect the functionality clinicians need into one contextual clinical workflow.

Product north star: Reduce clinician effort around the clinical decision, without taking the decision away from the clinician.

Discovery approach

I explored the Arog QA environment across dashboard, patient and clinical information, consultation, prescription, scheduling and operational workflows. I paired this with a short structured survey of three practicing clinicians across a solo practice, a multi-doctor clinic and a hospital. One medical student response was treated separately and not included in the clinician sample.

What surfaced

  • Orient: Clinicians need a useful view of today's appointments, waiting patients and remaining workload.
  • Understand: Previous visit notes, medications, diagnoses and relevant history should be available in the patient context.
  • Complete: Consultation documentation should support structured options and the clinician's own words, with review and sign-off remaining with the doctor.

In the early survey, all three clinicians wanted today's appointments and waiting patients surfaced first, and all three wanted previous visit notes readily available. Two of three identified note-writing as a time burden and preferred a mix of structured options and free text.

Proposed connected workflow

Today's queue → patient workspace → consultation → review and sign → downstream communication, follow-up and billing.

The product principle is capture once, reuse appropriately, generate downstream artifacts. The doctor remains responsible for clinical decisions and confirmation; staff and the system support operational steps.

Prioritisation

  • Now: Clinician workspace, role-specific queue, longitudinal patient context and consultation workflow.
  • Next: Investigation comparison where structured result data is available.
  • Validate first: Treatment reuse and follow-up handling.
  • Later: Voice and AI assistance, subject to data quality, safety, governance and clinician validation.

Roadmap and dependencies

  • 0–3 months — Foundation: Clinician workspace, role-specific queue, patient context and consultation workflow.
  • 3–6 months — Connected care: Investigation comparison and validated follow-up handling.
  • 6–12 months — Assistance exploration: Clinician-specific shortcuts, voice and documentation support only after the foundation is reliable.

These are proposed sequencing horizons, not committed delivery dates. Data quality, patient/encounter identity, role-based permissions, auditability, specialty differences and legacy-data integration need engineering validation.

Success measures

Baseline before setting targets: documentation time per consultation, time to consultation-ready, duplicate data entry, encounters completed in the workspace, follow-up context usage, prescription corrections, documentation errors and workflow abandonment.

What this work demonstrates

Clinical workflow analysis, lightweight clinician discovery, evidence-to-opportunity synthesis, prioritisation, roadmap sequencing, technical dependency awareness and clinical-safety boundaries.

Evidence note: The clinician sample was three people. Findings are directional, not statistically representative. Business impact and roadmap outcomes have not been validated or delivered.
