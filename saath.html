Saath — support through the difficult moments.

A bilingual digital health coach concept for adults in India trying to stop smokeless tobacco. The product focuses on personal triggers, in-the-moment craving support and non-judgmental lapse recovery.

INDEPENDENT PRODUCT CONCEPT

01 / SAATH

The hard part of quitting often begins after the decision.

For many adults trying to stop smokeless tobacco, cravings collide with familiar routines — after meals, with tea or coffee, during stress, boredom, work or social situations. A lapse can feel like failure and lead someone to abandon the attempt.

Product thesis

Saath is a bilingual behavioural coach for Indian adults trying to stop smokeless tobacco. Rather than relying only on education, motivation or a quit date, it focuses on the moments when support is most needed.

Core loop: Understand → Prepare → Intervene → Learn → Adapt.

Designing around the critical moment

  • Understand: Capture language, motivation, tobacco type, previous quit attempts and the role tobacco plays in daily routines.
  • Prepare: Map high-risk triggers and create a personalised quit plan.
  • Intervene: Make craving support immediately accessible through a clear “I want tobacco right now” action and one practical, context-specific intervention.
  • Learn: Ask whether the intervention helped and use the response as a personalisation signal.
  • Adapt: Re-rank useful strategies and support a non-judgmental return after a lapse.

MVP scope

  • English and Hindi onboarding
  • Motivation, tobacco-type and previous-attempt mapping
  • Trigger and routine mapping
  • Personalised quit plan
  • Home dashboard and one-tap craving support
  • Short intervention and outcome feedback
  • Progress tracking and lapse recovery

Clinical and AI safety

Saath is a coaching product, not a diagnostic or prescribing system. The proposed content architecture is: clinical guidelines and Indian evidence → expert-reviewed intervention library → user context → deterministic personalisation rules → bilingual coaching. Any future AI should personalise delivery within reviewed content rather than generate clinical advice.

How I would measure value

The proposed north-star is the proportion of recorded high-risk urges that users report managing without tobacco. This is an early behavioural signal, not a substitute for clinical cessation outcomes. Supporting measures include 7-, 30- and 90-day abstinence, time to first lapse, lapse recovery, craving-support activation, intervention helpfulness and safety guardrails.

Validation plan

The first research sprint proposes 8–12 interviews with repeated quitters, five onboarding usability tests, five craving-flow usability tests and clinician review of intervention and escalation content.

What this work demonstrates

Problem framing around a specific behavioural moment, a narrow MVP wedge, trigger-level personalisation, explicit safety boundaries, measurable hypotheses and a product loop that learns from user feedback.

Status and evidence: Independent product assignment / concept and UI proposal. No clinical efficacy or cessation outcomes are claimed. Proposed research and success measures have not yet been completed.
