Sakhi — a more personal
women's health experience.

A goal-led women's health experience spanning menstrual cycle tracking, pregnancy preparation and perimenopause / menopause support. This case study focuses on the onboarding experience visible in the shared MVP screen.

PRODUCT CASE STUDY

01 / OVERVIEW

Start with what matters to the user.

Women's health needs vary across life stages and personal circumstances. A generic experience can make it harder for users to find the tools and information relevant to them. Sakhi explores a more focused starting point: understand the user's primary health goal, then tailor the experience around that context.

The product approach: goal-led onboarding

The onboarding screen asks users to select a primary focus area after providing general health information. It presents three journeys:

01 · Track My Cycle

Period predictions, symptom tracking, daily period logging and PBAC calculations.

02 · Prepare for Pregnancy

Ovulation tracking, fertility insights, daily cycle logging and trying-to-conceive support.

03 · Perimenopause / Menopause

Symptom tracking, treatment options, lifestyle guidance and expert insights.

Key product decisions

  • Start with the user's goal: organise the experience around a primary need rather than presenting every feature at once.
  • Allow the experience to evolve: users can add additional goals later from the dashboard.
  • Keep the choice reversible: users can change their focus in settings, reducing pressure to make a permanent decision during onboarding.
  • Set expectations: the next step is described as goal-specific setup and preferences.

What this screen demonstrates

  • Goal-based user segmentation and personalisation.
  • Progressive disclosure of product functionality.
  • User control and flexibility.
  • Translation of women's health use cases into distinct product journeys.

Clinical and product considerations

Cycle predictions, fertility insights, PBAC calculations and treatment-related content require careful handling of medical evidence, privacy and user expectations. These capabilities should be clinically reviewed, appropriately qualified and validated before being presented as medical guidance. The product should support informed engagement with health information, not imply diagnosis or replace professional medical advice.

Evidence note: This page is based on the onboarding screen shared for the portfolio. It documents the interface and product decisions visible in that screen; it does not claim that every underlying capability is fully implemented, clinically validated or supported by measured outcomes.

What I would validate next

  • Whether users understand the three goal options and can identify the right starting point.
  • Whether the goal-led approach improves onboarding completion and perceived relevance.
  • Clinical accuracy of calculations, predictions and educational content.
  • Privacy, consent and appropriate handling of sensitive health data.
  • Feature engagement and retention after users complete goal-specific setup.
